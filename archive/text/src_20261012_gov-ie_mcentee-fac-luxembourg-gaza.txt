The Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade, Helen McEntee TD, today attended a meeting of EU Foreign Ministers in Luxembourg.
Ministers discussed a range of issues, including Russian aggression against Ukraine; the situation in the Middle East; hybrid threats and European security challenges.
Speaking after the meeting, the Minister said: “Today’s meeting was an important opportunity to advance a number of key Irish Presidency priorities. We saw the adoption of the single largest sanctions listings to date, almost 1600 additional listings under the Ukraine Territorial Integrity sanctions regime. This is a really important signal of our determination to keep the pressure on Russia. As we heard from Ukrainian Foreign Minister Sybiha, Russia continues to show no interest in peace and in fact continues to escalate its indiscriminate missile and drone attacks against Ukrainian cities.
“We have also seen significant progress regarding Ukraine’s accession path which is another key priority for our Presidency. Ukraine’s future is in the EU and I strongly welcome the progress being made. Following the Ukrainian parliament’s adoption of the draft law on education today, we will take steps to allow us open clusters 2 and 3 with both Ukraine and Moldova at an tomorrow. This is a further important step. An Inter-Governmental Conference with Montenegro to close three chapters also took place today. And tomorrow we will close one chapter with Albania. We are committed to making as much progress as possible before the end of the year.
“On Lebanon, I am delighted that we have agreed the establishment of an EU Common Security and Defence Policy Support Mission. This was also a key priority for our Presidency and it is an important demonstration of our commitment to Lebanon and its people. The EU must do all it can to contribute to peace and security in Lebanon.
“On Israel/Palestine, we are continuing to witness a humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza with Palestinians now facing a fourth winter in dire circumstances. Settlement expansion, including the illegal West Bank settlement project, and associated settler violence in the occupied West Bank are without precedent. Our immediate efforts on Gaza must remain focused on rapid and unimpeded passage of humanitarian aid.
“Together with a number of EU colleagues, I reiterated the call for action at EU level on the issue of trade with settlements. The ongoing and egregious Israeli breaches of human rights and unilateral actions that undermine the two State solution require concrete action to be taken at the EU level. The two-state Solution is the only path to secure lasting peace between Israelis and Palestinians, and for the wider region. I note with regret the postponement of legislative elections in Palestine and hope that these can take place as soon as possible.
“We discussed the situation in the Gulf and the wider Middle East, which remains deeply concerning. I condemned the attack by the Houthis on King Khalid Airport in Riyadh which has killed and injured many civilians. We stand in solidarity with Saudi Arabia in the face of this threat. We also discussed the Gulf conflict and international efforts to restore safe and toll-free transit passage through the straits, in line with international law. Only dialogue and diplomacy can bring lasting stability and security to the region.
“Finally, I reiterated Ireland’s full support for the independence and vital role of the International Criminal Court following the announcement of measures against the court by the US.”
