Prime Minister and Minister for Finance Lawrence Wong and Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese held the 11th Singapore-Australia Annual Leaders’ Meeting (ALM) in Canberra today.
The Prime Ministers reaffirmed the excellent state of bilateral relations and welcomed the good progress made under the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership (CSP) 2.0 since its establishment in October 2025. They underscored the importance of strengthening resilience and connectivity among trusted partners amid an increasingly uncertain global environment. Key outcomes of the ALM included the signing of the landmark Agreement on Transboundary Movement of Carbon Dioxide Streams for the Purposes of Offshore Geological Sequestration; the signing of a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) on Essential Supplies, which builds on the Protocol to the Singapore-Australia Free Trade Agreement on Economic Resilience and Essential Supplies signed in July 2026 to strengthen supply chains; and the upcoming launch of the Getting to Zero Co-Innovation Programme to support joint development and commercialisation of low-emission solutions.
The Prime Ministers also welcomed further progress in defence and security cooperation, and discussed ways to enhance cooperation in artificial intelligence (AI), the green and digital economies. They welcomed the commencement of Singapore Airlines flights between Singapore and Western Sydney International (WSI) Airport on 23 November 2026, which will help to enhance people-to-people ties.
The Prime Ministers reaffirmed their shared commitment to deepening economic integration between Australia and Southeast Asia, as part of efforts to build a more seamlessly connected region. They discussed opportunities to advance this goal through closer trade and investment ties. Prime Minister Wong welcomed Australia’s support for Singapore’s 2027 ASEAN Chairmanship. Both Prime Ministers also agreed on the importance of free and open trade, an open and inclusive regional architecture, and a rules-based international order.
Following the ALM, the Prime Ministers issued a Joint Statement on the key outcomes of their meeting and a Joint Statement on cooperation on AI. The Joint Statements are appended.
Prime Minister Wong also met the Leader of the Opposition Angus Taylor in Canberra today. During the meeting, they affirmed the strength of bilateral relations, and the importance of Australia’s continued engagement of ASEAN. They also discussed issues like AI and energy security.
In Sydney, Prime Minister Wong will attend a reception for Overseas Singaporeans in the evening, and will call on Governor-General of the Commonwealth of Australia Sam Mostyn on 25 November 2026. Prime Minister Wong will depart Australia for Singapore on 25 November 2026.

SINGAPORE

Annex
JOINT STATEMENT FOR THE 11TH SINGAPORE-AUSTRALIA ANNUAL LEADERS’ MEETING
Introduction
1 The Prime Minister and Minister for Finance of Singapore, Lawrence Wong, and the Prime Minister of Australia, the Hon Anthony Albanese MP, met for the 11th Singapore-Australia Annual Leaders’ Meeting in Canberra, on the traditional land of the Ngunnawal peoples, on 24 November 2026.
2 In the past year, Australia and Singapore have advanced an ambitious agenda under the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership 2.0, delivering tangible outcomes for the prosperity, security, connectivity and resilience of both countries and the region. Leaders welcomed strengthened economic and security cooperation, enhanced supply chain resilience, expanded partnerships in emerging domains including AI, and the signing of a first-of-its-kind agreement for our two countries on the transboundary movement of carbon dioxide streams for offshore geological sequestration. Building on complementary strengths and deep strategic trust, the partnership demonstrates the interdependence of our two countries, our strong people-to-people links and the strong momentum to work together across established and emerging areas of cooperation to address shared challenges.
Contributing to Peace and Stability, At Home and In Our Region
3 Australia and Singapore share an enduring commitment to the peace, stability, prosperity and resilience of Southeast Asia and the broader Indo-Pacific. Leaders reaffirmed their support for an effective, inclusive and rules-based multilateral system, international law, including the United Nations Charter, and a region that supports free and open trade, dialogue, restraint, transparency and the peaceful resolution of disputes in accordance with international law.
4 Leaders acknowledged the close and longstanding defence and security partnership and its important contribution to regional peace and security. They welcomed ongoing progress to enhance our respective militaries’ reciprocal access to defence facilities in Australia and Singapore; deepen and increase the complexity of defence science and technology collaboration; strengthen our defence supply chain resilience; and expand professional exchanges and collaboration between our militaries, in line with the 2025 Memorandum of Understanding concerning Enhanced Defence Cooperation. Leaders welcomed the substantive conclusion of negotiations to renew the Oakey Treaty, which underpins more than 25 years of RSAF helicopter training activities at Oakey, Queensland. Leaders reaffirmed their strong commitment to the Five Power Defence Arrangements (FPDA) and commended efforts to deepen cooperation on defence capability, industry, science and technology, including joint experimentation at Exercise TRIDENT 2026. They committed to strengthening practical defence cooperation and strategic dialogue through ASEAN-led mechanisms, including the ASEAN Defence Ministers’ Meeting Plus.
5 Leaders welcomed the continued expansion of security and policing cooperation to respond to shared security challenges and risks to national resilience, as demonstrated by the 2nd Singapore-Australia Security Dialogue that was recently held on 14 October 2026 in Singapore. They welcomed progress on the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on Cooperation in Security Matters and the Border of the Future Partnership. Leaders also welcomed bilateral cooperation in the region, including joint cyber incident training for Southeast Asian partners and the inaugural Australia-Singapore Strategic Communications Dialogue.
6 Leaders reaffirmed Australia and Singapore’s commitment to supporting the rules-based multilateral system to deliver on its core functions and mandates, and to uphold the rules, norms and institutions that underpin prosperity and security in our region. Leaders welcomed progress on UN reform and encouraged continued efforts to build a UN that delivers better. Leaders reaffirmed their commitment to WTO reform and ongoing cooperation on electronic commerce.
7 Leaders also affirmed the importance of working closely with partners in ASEAN to promote a stable, peaceful and prosperous region. Leaders welcomed cooperation under the Singapore-Australia Third Country Training Programme to support capacity-building in Southeast Asia to enhance the region’s resilience.
8 Leaders reaffirmed their support for implementing commitments in the ASEAN-Australia Joint Leaders’ Statement on Conflict Prevention and Crisis Management in the ASEAN-led Regional Architecture. Leaders also reaffirmed their commitment to conflict prevention, including through capacity-building workshops, expert exchanges, and working with regional partners to encourage dialogue and build trust, maintain open channels of communication, and reduce the risks of escalation, crisis and conflict. The second Track 1.5 Workshop on Conflict Prevention was held in November 2026 in Canberra.
9 Leaders also looked forward to further progress towards concluding a Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty to establish a framework for cooperation between the two countries in providing legal assistance in criminal matters.
Connecting Our Economies
10 Leaders reaffirmed Australia and Singapore’s shared commitment to deepening economic integration and strengthening economic resilience in the face of growing geoeconomic uncertainty. Through the upgraded Singapore-Australia Free Trade Agreement (SAFTA) and Agreement Establishing the ASEAN-Australia-New Zealand Free Trade Area (AANZFTA), and shared engagement in the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) and the Indo-Pacific Economic Framework for Prosperity (IPEF), Australia and Singapore are supporting businesses, strengthening supply chain resilience and unlocking opportunities in emerging sectors. Leaders also reaffirmed the Singapore-Australia Digital Economy Agreement as a cornerstone of the modern economic partnership. Leaders noted the potential of the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) and welcomed ASEAN’s leadership in harnessing the opportunities of the digital economy. Leaders committed to further collaboration to promote growth, resilience and competitiveness.
11 Leaders welcomed the signing of the Protocol to SAFTA on Economic Resilience and Essential Supplies on 27 July 2026 and the MoU on Essential Supplies on 24 November 2026. The Protocol reflects the priority Australia and Singapore accord each other with respect to trade in essential supplies and also recognises the complementarity, connectivity and interdependence of our two economies. Leaders also welcomed the inaugural Economic Resilience Dialogue in Singapore on 5 November 2026, which discussed approaches towards supply chain resilience, research security, technology and innovation, and investment screening.
12 Leaders welcomed cooperation to create new opportunities for trade, investment and sustainable economic growth across Southeast Asia, including through Singapore’s connectivity with the region and Invested: Australia’s Southeast Asia Economic Strategy to 2040. They welcomed practical initiatives that support this objective, including Australia’s Investment Deal Team in Singapore and Australian investment in Singapore’s FAST-P initiative to support the region’s development and clean energy transition. They also welcomed continued cooperation on food security and supply resilience through the Australia-Singapore Food Pact, including progress on protocols for the Simplified Transshipment Model to facilitate the distribution of Australian red meat through Singapore to the wider region.
13 Leaders also welcomed strengthened aviation connectivity, including the inaugural Singapore Airlines flight to Western Sydney International Airport. Both sides are also committed to exploring opportunities to further strengthen connectivity. Our aviation authorities will continue discussions to review the Singapore-Australia Air Services Agreement.
Transitioning to Net-Zero
14 Australia and Singapore have significantly deepened bilateral energy cooperation. Both sides recognised the important role each country plays in the other’s energy security and agreed to work closely together to advance ambitious action and practical outcomes on energy transition and electrification. Leaders welcomed the inaugural Ministerial Dialogue on Energy, during which both countries reaffirmed their commitment to remaining reliable suppliers of energy to each other. The leaders welcomed the signing of the first-of-its-kind Agreement on Transboundary Movement of Carbon Dioxide Streams for the Purposes of Offshore Geological Sequestration. The Agreement advances regional decarbonisation and reflects our pathfinding approach to the transition to net-zero.
15 The Singapore-Australia Green Economy Agreement remains a cornerstone of the bilateral partnership on climate, energy and economic transformation. Leaders reaffirmed their commitment to practical outcomes that strengthen energy security, accelerate decarbonisation and unlock new economic opportunities, including through co-innovation grant programs under the Getting to Zero initiative and work on the Australia-Singapore Green and Digital Shipping Corridor, as well as enhancing grid interconnectivity in line with the bilateral Cross-Border Electricity Trade Framework between Australia and Singapore.
Forging New Frontiers
16 Australia and Singapore are expanding cooperation in critical and emerging technologies, science and innovation to address shared challenges and seize new opportunities. Leaders acknowledged the need for greater international cooperation on responsible and trustworthy AI adoption and announced a Joint Statement on Cooperation on AI, reflecting both countries’ desire to work together to support the development and deployment of safe, secure, responsible and effective AI, and to shape AI governance and advance international standards. Leaders determined to exchange experiences in developing multilingual AI, taking reference from Singapore’s SEA-LION and Australia’s effort to support Pacific-led AI capabilities that reflect the region’s languages, cultures and priorities.
17 Leaders welcomed progress under the inaugural Australia-Singapore Cyber and Digital Senior Officials’ Dialogue held in July 2026, and the refreshed MoU on Cyber Security Cooperation. They looked forward to building on this momentum through the Australia-Singapore Cyber Operations Cooperation Action Plan to further strengthen bilateral cyber cooperation, as underpinned by the CSP 2.0.
18 Leaders welcomed enduring and expanded cooperation, including with ASEAN and industry, to promote international best practice for the security and resilience of submarine cables, which are global infrastructure crucial for digital connectivity, energy security and economic growth.
19 Leaders welcomed growing cooperation in space, including through the inaugural Australia-Singapore Space Dialogue held in July 2026, and reaffirmed continued engagement on shared health challenges, pandemic preparedness and regional health security. Leaders acknowledged the recently signed health genomics MoU between Genomics Australia and Precision Health Research Singapore (PRECISE) which will facilitate collaboration and knowledge exchange on further embedding genomics into our respective healthcare systems.
Deepening Friendships and Capabilities
20 Leaders acknowledged that education, research and innovation are central pillars of the Australia-Singapore partnership and key drivers of future prosperity and resilience. Enduring people-to-people links forged through study, research, professional exchanges, business, culture and the arts continue to strengthen the bonds between our countries. Leaders recognised the value of the renewal of the Australia-Singapore Arts Group for the third term, Australia Awards and New Colombo Plan, welcomed the inaugural intake of Australia Awards scholars from Singapore in 2026, and encouraged closer links between universities, greater two-way student exchange and continued research collaboration. They reaffirmed their commitment to cooperation and exchanges to strengthen public service capabilities and delivery in both countries, including through the Australia-Singapore Public Service Roundtable of which the 10th edition was held in Canberra in October 2026.
Global and Regional Developments
21 Leaders reaffirmed their shared commitment to international law and regional stability. They emphasised the primacy of the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), which sets out the legal framework within which all activities in the oceans and seas must be carried out. They noted the importance of upholding all navigational rights and freedoms, including the right of unimpeded transit passage in straits used for international navigation, in accordance with international law as reflected in UNCLOS, and reaffirmed their commitment to security and stability in the South China Sea and the peaceful resolution of disputes in accordance with international law. Leaders emphasised that any Code of Conduct in the South China Sea should be consistent with international law, particularly UNCLOS, and not prejudice the legitimate rights and interests of all nations.
22 The Leaders reaffirmed their shared interest in a peaceful, stable and prosperous Southeast Asia. Singapore looked forward to working closely with ASEAN Member States and ASEAN’s external partners including Australia to deliver substantive and meaningful outcomes as ASEAN Chair in 2027. The Leaders welcomed the adoption of the ASEAN – Australia Joint Leaders’ Statement commemorating the fifth anniversary of the ASEAN–Australia Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, and reaffirmed their commitment to advancing its implementation. The Leaders reaffirmed their commitment to uphold ASEAN Centrality and the ASEAN-led regional architecture, including the East Asia Summit, ASEAN Regional Forum, and ASEAN Defence Ministers’ Meeting Plus. They expressed their continued support for the practical implementation of the ASEAN Outlook on the Indo-Pacific (AOIP), through tangible projects under the four priority areas of the AOIP. The Leaders also reaffirmed their commitment to working together through the ASEAN-Australia Comprehensive Strategic Partnership to advance regional integration and strengthen cooperation in areas of mutual interest.
23 Leaders expressed deep concern at the ongoing crisis and dire humanitarian situation in Myanmar, called for an immediate cessation of violence and full implementation of ASEAN’s Five-Point Consensus, and supported ASEAN’s efforts to seek a resolution. They called on Russia to end its war against Ukraine and reiterated the need for a comprehensive, just and lasting peace that respects sovereignty and territorial integrity.
24 Leaders reaffirmed support for a two-State solution as the only viable path for Israelis and Palestinians to live in enduring peace, security and dignity. They expressed deep concern over the deteriorating situation in the West Bank, including escalating settler violence, and the humanitarian situation in Gaza, and reiterated their call for all parties to facilitate the flow of humanitarian aid into Gaza. They also called for the swift implementation of the Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict, consistent with the relevant UNSC resolutions. Leaders called for safe, secure and unimpeded transit passage through the Strait of Hormuz in accordance with international law, particularly UNCLOS.
25 The Leaders reaffirmed their commitment to advancing global climate action and the clean energy transition. In particular, Leaders welcomed growing resolve to address overshoot to keep 1.5°C within reach, and ambition to increase clean energy as a bigger share of global energy consumption.

JOINT STATEMENT ON COOPERATION ON ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE BY MR LAWRENCE WONG, PRIME MINISTER OF SINGAPORE AND THE HON ANTHONY ALBANESE, PRIME MINISTER OF AUSTRALIA
1 On the occasion of the 11th Singapore-Australia Annual Leaders’ Meeting, the Prime Ministers of Australia and Singapore underscored their shared commitment to deepen cooperation on Artificial Intelligence (AI) to further strengthen the Australia–Singapore Comprehensive Strategic Partnership 2.0.
2 Leaders welcomed the inaugural Australia–Singapore Cyber and Digital Senior Officials’ Dialogue held in July 2026 and reaffirmed cooperation under the 2024 Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on Artificial Intelligence Cooperation and the refreshed 2026 MoU on Cyber Security Cooperation.
3 Leaders recognised the transformative opportunities that AI presents to improve lives, support and create employment, increase productivity and advance research and innovation. They expressed their ambition to seize and shape these opportunities and share the benefits to foster inclusive and sustainable economic growth for our countries and the region. They affirmed their ambition to deploy AI to improve our peoples’ lives by accelerating AI adoption across business and government to enhance service delivery, support innovation-led growth and position Australia and Singapore as trusted AI hubs for the Indo-Pacific.
4 Leaders emphasised their shared commitment to support the development and deployment of safe, secure, responsible and effective AI. They agreed that AI should boost productivity and strengthen economic resilience while recognising the importance of mitigating risks, including ensuring that AI remains under human direction, oversight and control.
5 Leaders agreed to deepen bilateral cooperation through exchanges on AI testing, evaluation and assurance, and workforce capability and skills development, as well as by strengthening business-government links, to promote AI adoption. They committed to exploring greater interoperability, including in digital infrastructure and cross-border data flows, and strengthening collaboration between research institutes to turn innovation into commercial opportunities.
6 Leaders committed to cooperate on international efforts to shape AI governance and advance international standards, including via platforms such as the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science. They affirmed that the Indo-Pacific region should have a voice in shaping AI’s development to ensure that the region’s diverse needs, priorities and values are reflected in international AI governance frameworks.
7 Leaders committed to cooperate on emerging cyber and AI security challenges. They recognised the importance of ensuring trusted cross-border data flows and strengthening data governance frameworks to enable innovation while safeguarding privacy and security. They acknowledged the importance of building public trust to allow AI to be adopted with confidence, including through implementing safeguards and preparing for AI-enabled emerging threats.
8 Leaders welcomed opportunities to share experiences in developing multilingual AI that reflect local languages, cultures and priorities. They agreed to explore opportunities to support Pacific-led AI capabilities, drawing on lessons learnt from the development of SEA-LION and initiatives such as Singapore’s Project Atlas.
9 Leaders reaffirmed their strong support for ASEAN Centrality and recognised Singapore’s 2027 ASEAN Chair Year as an opportunity to advance practical cooperation on AI governance, including on AI testing standards and methodologies, in support of the ASEAN Working Group on AI Governance’s efforts to build a trusted, secure, resilient and interoperable regional AI ecosystem.
