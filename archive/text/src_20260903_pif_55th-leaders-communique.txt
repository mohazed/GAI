FORUM COMMUNIQUÉ
The Fifty-Fifth Pacific Islands Forum was held in Koror, Republic of Palau from 31 August
– 3 September 2026 and was attended by the Heads of State and Government of Australia, Cook
Islands, Federated States of Micronesia, French Polynesia, the Republic of the Marshall Islands,
the Republic of Naoero, New Zealand, Niue, the Republic of Palau, the Independent State of Papua
New Guinea, Solomon Islands, Kingdom of Tonga and Tuvalu. The Republic of Fiji, New
Caledonia, Vanuatu and Samoa were represented at Ministerial level, and the Republic of Kiribati
at Senior Official level. The Forum Leaders Retreat was held at Tab, Ngarchelong in Palau.
2. American Samoa, Guam and Tokelau attended the formal session as Associate Members.
The Council of Regional Organisations of the Pacific (CROP) in attendance were: Pacific Aviation
Safety Office (PASO), Pacific Community (SPC), Pacific Island Development Program (PIDP),
Pacific Islands Forum Fisheries Agency (FFA), Pacific Power Association (PPA), Pacific Tourism
Organisation (SPTO), and the Secretariat of the Pacific Regional Environment Programme
(SPREP). The Office of the Pacific Ocean Commissioner (OPOC) was also in attendance. Forum
Leaders also welcomed the opportunity to engage with the broader Forum family, including
representatives from civil society and the private sector during the formal session of Leaders.
3. Leaders expressed their warm appreciation to His Excellency Surangel Whipps Jr.,
President of the Republic of Palau and the Government and people of Palau, for the kind and
generous hospitality extended to all delegations. Leaders commended the outstanding
preparations and facilitation of the 2026 Leaders and related meetings which enabled Leaders and
all participants to engage in inclusive and constructive discussions, and contributed to the overall
success of the 55th Pacific Islands Forum.
4. Leaders also acknowledged the outgoing Chair of the Pacific Islands Forum, the
Honourable Matthew Wale, Prime Minister of Solomon Islands and his predecessor the
Honourable Jeremiah Manele, and the Government and people of Solomon Islands for their
exemplary leadership since the 54th Pacific Islands Forum, and during their chairing tenure.
B.E.L.A.U - BUILDING ECONOMIES: LIFE. ACTION. UNITY
5. Leaders commended the Republic of Palau for the 55th Pacific Islands Forum theme,
B.E.L.A.U. - Building Economies: Life. Action. Unity., recognising its vision for a resilient,
inclusive and sustainable region. Leaders welcomed the theme’s emphasis on investing in people,
cultures and communities, advancing Pacific regionalism and regional integration, accelerating
implementation of regional priorities, and strengthening unity and collective action in response to
shared challenges and opportunities. Leaders affirmed that through stronger regional cooperation
and solidarity, the Blue Pacific can build economies that expand opportunities for all while
protecting the region's people, environment and cultural heritage for future generations.
6. Leaders further affirmed the 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent as the region's
overarching long-term vision for advancing a resilient, integrated, secure and prosperous Blue
Pacific. Leaders endorsed the Third Annual Report on the Implementation of the 2050 Strategy

and welcomed progress across its thematic areas and Regional Collective Actions. Leaders
underscored the importance of sustained political leadership, strengthened regional coordination,
and effective partnerships to accelerate implementation and deliver tangible outcomes for Pacific
peoples.
REGIONAL AND STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENT
7. Leaders acknowledged that the Blue Pacific continues to face a period of growing
uncertainty, marked by intensifying geopolitical competition, economic volatility, climate threats
and rapid technological change. Leaders noted that these global developments increasingly affect
Pacific economies, societies and development pathways, reinforcing the importance of a united
and strategic regional response. Leaders reaffirmed that regional priorities and aspirations must
remain at the centre of the region's engagement with international partners and institutions.
8. Leaders emphasised that a stronger and more integrated Blue Pacific is essential to
navigating an increasingly complex world. Leaders reaffirmed their commitment to regionalism
as the primary vehicle for advancing collective priorities, strengthening resilience and amplifying
Pacific influence on the global stage. Leaders underscored that Pacific regionalism is
strengthened by the sovereignty of Members and their collective right to determine their own
priorities, shape their own agenda and choose their own partnerships. Leaders called for sustained
political leadership and cooperation to ensure the region remains united, capable and responsive
to the needs of Pacific peoples acknowledging the Forum’s expanding coordination role and
underscoring that the regional architecture, and the resourcing available to it must keep pace with
that role. Leaders also acknowledged the need to continue to advocate for and support the Smaller
Island States (SIS), including the advancement of the SIS priorities in partnership with CROP
agencies.
POLITICAL LEADERSHIP AND REGIONALISM
9. Leaders reaffirmed that strong political leadership and effective regionalism were
essential to advancing the collective interests of the Blue Pacific. Leaders welcomed progress in
the implementation of the Review of the Regional Architecture (RRA), noting its contribution to
strengthening regional governance, enhancing coordination across the regional system, and
improving the capacity of Pacific institutions to deliver on the priorities of Leaders. Leaders also
endorsed the revised Communiqué drafting procedures as an important measure to reinforce
Member ownership of Forum decision-making and to strengthen transparency and consistency.
10. Leaders underscored the importance and value of partners to the region and reaffirmed
that the delivery of the 2050 Strategy rests on partnerships that are genuine, durable, and
accountable, which strengthen Pacific leadership, voice, and engagement to ensure recognition and
alignment with Pacific cultures, values, priorities, and collective interests.
11. Leaders reaffirmed the 2025 Leaders Policy on Partnerships, and in progressing the
operationalisation of the new partnership mechanism, agreed on the placement of partners in the
two partnership streams, subject to a periodic review in one year that is informed by an
accountability and reporting framework, which is to be implemented from 2027. Leaders further
agreed that the Secretary General would communicate the decision to Partners in line with the
endorsed communication approach.

4
12. Leaders welcomed the comprehensive report of the Troika Review Committee which built
on the 2025 High-Level Political Talanoa Report. Leaders endorsed the recommendations of the
Troika Review Committee for integration into the Regional Reform Agenda emerging from the
RRA and endorsed the phased approach of all the related RRA decisions to support the sequenced
consideration, resource implication, and ensuring the management of implementation in a manner
that does not disrupt regional processes. Leaders further agreed to consolidate these reforms and
the wider RRA decisions into a single transition and implementation pathway through to 2030
under the operational oversight of the Forum Troika, with periodic reporting to Forum Leaders.
13. Leaders recognised the importance of strengthening engagement between the Pacific
Islands Forum and regional parliamentary groupings, including the Pacific Inter-Parliamentary
Assembly, to promote greater political continuity and broader understanding and advocacy of
shared Pacific challenges. Leaders agreed that opportunities to strengthen this connection should
be considered through the ongoing RRA.
14. Leaders also recognised the importance of investing in the next generation of Pacific
leaders and strengthening links between regional governance and the Pacific’s academic, policy,
technical, and scientific communities. Leaders welcomed Tonga’s ARISE 2050 Initiative
(Advancing Resilient, Innovative and Sustainable Solutions for Emerging Leaders) pilot, aligned
with the 2050 Strategy and Pacific Centred Leadership Initiative (PCLI) endorsed by Leaders in
2025, and acknowledged its potential to strengthen the pipeline of emerging Pacific Public Sector
Leaders. Leaders recognised the value of merging ARISE and PCLI and directed the Secretariat
to progress this in the design of PCLI for consideration by Leaders in 2027.
15. In recognition of an increasingly complex regional and global environment, Leaders
emphasised the need for a more coherent and strategic approach to advancing Pacific interests
internationally. Leaders welcomed the ongoing work to develop the 2050 International
Engagement and Advocacy Strategy as a framework for strengthening regional advocacy,
partnerships, development cooperation, and resource mobilisation, in support of Members’
priorities for Leaders’ consideration in 2027.
16. Leaders recalled the global energy crisis which emerged in the first half of 2026 and
welcomed the agility of the regional system in responding collectively to resulting challenges
through the Regional Response Mechanism (RRM) under the Biketawa Declaration. Leaders
recognised the RRM’s value as a member-led mechanism strengthening regional preparedness,
resilience, and timely political decision-making, with technical and operational support from the
CROP taskforce. Leaders emphasised the importance of delivering tangible and timely results for
Members.
17. Leaders welcomed progress made to operationalise the Rarotonga Treaty and the
upcoming second Meeting of States Parties to be held alongside events to commemorate the 40th
anniversary of the Treaty’s entry into force in December 2026. Leaders reaffirmed the Pacific's
longstanding commitment to a peaceful and secure nuclear-free region, in particular one that is
free of nuclear weapons and nuclear testing. Leaders welcomed progress on the Preliminary
Independent Review on Nuclear Contamination in the Pacific, including at the Runit Nuclear Test
Site, and recalling the 2024 Leaders decision to assist the Republic of the Marshall Islands in its
engagement with the United States of America (U.S.) towards a just resolution of the U.S. Nuclear
Testing Programme, Leaders agreed to issue a joint letter to the U.S. taking into account the
information presented by the Preliminary Review.

18. Leaders further reaffirmed maintaining regional and international engagement on the
discharge of the Advanced Liquid Processing System treated water, including with the
International Atomic Energy Agency, and advancing long-term regional approaches to nuclear-
related contamination and discharge issues.
19. Leaders expressed deep concern at rising global nuclear risks, including expanding nuclear
arsenals, increasing tensions among nuclear-armed states, and the erosion of international
disarmament and arms control frameworks. Leaders noted with concern the inability of the 2026
Review Conference of the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT) to reach a
substantive outcome.
20. Leaders congratulated the Kingdom of Tonga on its accession to the Treaty on the
Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons and ratification of the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty
(CTBT), completing the universalisation of the CTBT in the Pacific, and reaffirmed their
unwavering commitment to the CTBT as underpinning the global norm against nuclear weapons
testing across the Pacific region. Leaders also expressed their grave concern about growing
nuclear weapons stockpiles and called on all states to advance the shared goal of a world free of
nuclear weapons.
New Caledonia
21. Leaders welcomed the update on the recent political development provided by New

further reaffirmed the Forum's attentive role in continuing to engage with New Caledonia on its
political, economic and social situation and on monitoring the self-determination process in New

reaffirmed the support for sustained inclusive dialogue among all stakeholders towards a peaceful
political transition. Leaders noted the outcomes of the Melanesian Spearhead Group Special

West Papua (Papua)

2023 and 2025 on the appointment of Special Envoys, including the 2018 invitation by Indonesia
to the UN Human Rights Commissioner, and agreed to facilitate a dialogue with Indonesia on
areas of shared interest for mutual understanding. Leaders further agreed to facilitate a mission
with interested Leaders, in consultation with the Melanesian Spearhead Group Secretariat, and
report back to Forum Leaders in 2027.

PEACE AND SECURITY
25. Leaders reaffirmed the Boe Declaration on Regional Security and the Blue Pacific Ocean
of Peace Declaration as the region's principal frameworks to advance a comprehensive and people-
centred approach to peace and security. Leaders further reaffirmed the expanded concept of
security and that climate change remains the single greatest threat to the livelihoods, security and
wellbeing of Pacific peoples and communities. Leaders underscored the importance of collective
action to address traditional and non-traditional security challenges, including geopolitical
tensions, transnational organised crime, climate change, economic disruption and emerging
strategic risks. Leaders endorsed the Regional Peace and Security Action Plan 2026-2030 to
advance regional peace and security priorities and emphasised the importance of Member-led
implementation that is responsive to differing national circumstances and capacities, appropriately
prioritised, and adequately resourced.
26. Leaders endorsed the Pacific Humanitarian Response Coordination Mechanism
(PResCoM) Policy and Operating Guidelines, recognising their importance in strengthening
regional humanitarian preparedness and response arrangements. Leaders also agreed in principle
to the Waqa Moana Concept as a regional mechanism to strengthen cooperation against
transnational organised crime and illicit drug trafficking through enhanced intelligence sharing,
coordination and maritime security cooperation, while noting that further national consultations
will be undertaken. Leaders further reiterated the need to address the social impacts of illicit
drugs and broader transnational crime through integrated approaches that encompass prevention,
treatment, rehabilitation and community resilience.
27. Leaders reaffirmed the centrality of Member-led regional governance processes and
recognised the diverse circumstances and competencies of Members. Leaders agreed in principle
to the inclusion of the Pacific Police Ministers Meeting within the Forum architecture, as well as
recognition of the Joint Heads of Pacific Security meeting within the regional peace and security
architecture. Leaders noted that further consultations will be undertaken on the associated
governance, institutional arrangements and composition, including through the RRA, and
emphasised that final arrangements should strengthen coherence, take account of the capacity and
resource implications for Members, complement existing regional mechanisms, and avoid
unnecessary duplication.
28. Leaders recognised the importance of strengthening regional preparedness and response
arrangements and noted ongoing discussions on the proposed Integrated Regional Response
Mechanism and Regional Operations Deployment Framework, acknowledging the need for further
consultations. Leaders also noted the proposal by the Government of Solomon Islands on the
establishment of a Blue Pacific Ocean of Peace Regional Security Treaty, encouraged its further
consideration by Members, and acknowledged the need to undertake further consultations.
29. Leaders, in recalling the Blue Pacific Ocean of Peace Declaration, registered concern on
the recent intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) test which overflew a number of Members
without adequate notice. Leaders called on all States undertaking ICBM testing to ensure
transparency and reassurance including through the provision of advance notification of at least

7
24 hours in accordance with international practice. Leaders emphasised the importance of all
States conducting ICBM tests to do so consistently with the Blue Pacific Ocean of Peace
Declaration, Treaty of Rarotonga and in respect of the sovereignty of Forum Members1.
RESOURCE AND ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
30. Leaders reaffirmed the Pacific Roadmap for Economic Development (PRED) as the
region's principal framework for advancing economic integration, recognising that stronger, more
diversified Pacific economies are essential to strengthening resilience, sustaining sovereignty, and
improving the prosperity and wellbeing of Pacific peoples. Recognising an increasingly uncertain
global economic environment, Leaders emphasised the importance of deeper regional
cooperation, greater economic diversification, improved connectivity, and stronger collective
capacity to respond to external shocks. Leaders also called for stronger recognition of the United
Nations Multi-Dimensional Vulnerability Index by International Financial Institutions,
reaffirming that gross national income alone does not reflect the true vulnerability of Forum
Island Countries. Leaders welcomed progress across key regional initiatives, including climate
and disaster risk finance, fisheries development, trade, labour mobility, and private sector
development, as important contributions to a more resilient and prosperous Blue Pacific.
31. Leaders welcomed and endorsed the KAVA Declaration, recognising KAVA as a unique
Pacific cultural and economic asset and reaffirming the importance of protecting its Pacific origins,
associated traditional knowledge, cultural identity and heritage. Leaders recognised the
Declaration as an important step towards strengthening Pacific ownership, enhancing value
addition and market opportunities, and safeguarding the long-term interests of Pacific producers
and communities in international trade.
32. Leaders welcomed the entry into force of the Agreement to Establish the Pacific Resilience
Facility on 6 May 2026 and acknowledged the convening of the inaugural Pacific Resilience
Facility (PRF) Council Meeting hosted and chaired by the Republic of the Marshall Islands.
Leaders welcomed progress towards the operationalisation of the PRF, supported the timely
appointment of the PRF Board, and further welcomed progress on community-impact initiatives.
33. Leaders reaffirmed the PRF as the region's pre-eminent climate finance investment
priority and called for enhanced regional advocacy to support the capitalisation, including through
engagement at UNGA81, Pre-COP meetings, and COP31.
34. Leaders commended the substantial progress towards the initial USD500 million
capitalisation target, including through existing pledges to the PRF valued at USD 162 million up
to the 54th Pacific Islands Forum. Leaders welcomed further pledges from: Solomon Island (USD
1m), Republic of the Marshall Islands (USD1m), Norway (USD1.05m), Palau (via GCF
readiness), Papua New Guinea (USD 4.5million), and the United Kingdom (USD14m), and called
on the international community to build on this through new and additional pledges that match the
ambition of the PRF that delivers for the Pacific communities now and into the future. Leaders
agreed that the full list of PRF pledges will be released immediately by the interim Board and
displayed prominently on the PIF-PRF website and updated at each Board meeting.
35. Leaders agreed that the Forum Troika Leaders, together with the Kingdom of Tonga and
Papua New Guinea, will lead the capitalisation efforts for the PRF.

36. Recognising fisheries as a strategic asset of the Blue Pacific, Leaders supported a phased,
Member-led consideration of the Regional Fisheries Development Fund feasibility assessment and
broader East New Britain Initiative-related work, informed by the Forum Fisheries Agency
process. Leaders emphasised the importance of increasing Pacific participation across the
fisheries value chain and maximising sustainable economic returns and benefits for Members,
while ensuring national ownership, economic accountability, equitable access, sustainability and
consistency with existing fisheries governance arrangements.
37. Leaders endorsed the establishment of a regional Labour Ministers Meeting as a dedicated
ministerial mechanism within the Forum architecture and associated Business Case and Ministerial
Charter, taking into account the ongoing review through the RRA. Leaders recognised that labour
and workforce governance have emerged as strategic regional priorities with increasing
implications for economic resilience, labour mobility, skills development, workforce sustainability
and inclusive growth. Leaders further recognised the complementarity of a Labour Ministers
Meeting with the Forum Economic Ministers Meeting (FEMM) and the Forum Trade Ministers
Meeting (FTMM), noting that FEMM provides oversight of regional economic policy and
integration, FTMM provides oversight of regional trade and labour mobility policy, and the Labour
Ministers Meeting would provide dedicated leadership on labour and workforce priorities. Leaders
emphasised that together, these ministerial mechanisms will strengthen policy coherence, regional
coordination and implementation of the Resources and Economic Development thematic area
priorities under the 2050 Strategy.
PEOPLE-CENTERED DEVELOPMENT
38. Leaders reaffirmed that people-centred development remains at the heart of the 2050
Strategy, recognising that investments in health, education, gender equality, disability inclusion
and human development are essential to the region's long-term resilience, prosperity and
wellbeing. Leaders reaffirmed the importance of human capital in the Pacific region and the need
to build capacity to support our development aspirations. Leaders welcomed progress across the
People-Centred Development thematic area and emphasised the importance of coordinated and
multi-sectoral approaches to address persistent development challenges and ensure that no one is
left behind.
39. Leaders endorsed the refreshed Pacific Framework for the Rights of Persons with
Disabilities, reaffirming their commitment to advancing disability rights, equity and inclusion
across the Blue Pacific. Leaders recognised the importance of strengthening the leadership and
participation of persons with disabilities in decision-making processes and emphasised the need
to remove structural barriers, strengthen inclusive systems and services, and translate regional
commitments into practical outcomes for persons with disabilities and their communities.
40. Leaders endorsed the outcomes of the 5th Pacific Islands Forum Women Leaders Meeting
and reaffirmed that gender equality remains fundamental to achieving the ambitions of the 2050
Strategy. Leaders further affirmed the need for sustainable financing for National Gender-Based
Violence (GBV) Prevention Frameworks as the primary constraint on scaling GBV prevention
progress and committed to ensuring that GBV prevention priorities should be reflected in national
planning, gender-responsive budgeting, and public investment decisions. Leaders further
recognised the opportunity to leverage climate and green finance to strengthen GBV prevention
efforts and called for the inclusion of gender responsive and inclusive measures in climate finance
proposals and resilience planning.

9
41. Leaders recognised the persistently low representation of women in national parliaments
across the region, reaffirmed the Revitalised Pacific Leaders Gender Equality Declaration and
agreed that inclusive leadership and in particular increasing women's political participation and
representation is needed to achieve progress across the 2050 Strategy. Leaders urged members to
adopt national policies and laws to increase women's political participation and representation.
Leaders welcomed the announcement by Australia on the investment of $195million in the Pacific
Women Lead Program, which has supported women’s leadership and entrepreneurship and
combatted gender-based violence.
42. Leaders recognised the development of an Early Childhood Development and Status of
Pacific Children Report in 2027 as an important opportunity to strengthen the evidence base for
child wellbeing and human development across the region. Leaders acknowledged that investment
in early childhood development delivers long-term social and economic benefits and contributes
to stronger and more resilient communities.
43. Leaders acknowledged the continuing burden of Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) as
one of the Pacific's most significant human development challenges and reaffirmed their
commitment to a whole-of-government and whole-of-society response to the Pacific NCD crisis.
Leaders called for accelerated implementation of the Pacific NCD Roadmap, recognising the
growing impact of NCDs on health outcomes, public expenditure, productivity and human capital
across the region.
44. Recognising that NCDs remain a significant development challenge with impacts on
health, productivity and economic resilience, Leaders reaffirmed support for strengthened
implementation of the Pacific NCD Roadmap, including through investments in prevention, early
intervention, health promotion and healthier communities. Leaders expressed concern over the
growing prevalence of emerging nicotine products, including e-cigarettes and nicotine pouches,
particularly their targeting of young people, and encouraged regional solutions to protect public
health and prevent the circumvention of existing safeguards.
45. Leaders reaffirmed the role of the Conference of Pacific Education Ministers to be hosted
by the Republic of Marshall Islands in 2027, as the oversight mechanism for the Review of the
Regional Education Architecture and welcomed ongoing efforts to further develop and implement
the second phase of the Pacific Regional Education Framework. Leaders recognised the
importance of strengthening regional education cooperation to address emerging challenges,
including climate change, technological transformation and workforce development, and
supported closer collaboration between the education and health sectors to promote the wellbeing
of Pacific learners.
46. Leaders also noted an update by the Government of Tonga on its whole-of-Government
approach in strengthening the reintegration of returning nationals including Tonga’s
encouragement of the need for broader regional implications of these policies, and to explore
practical cooperation on information sharing, reintegration support, and community resilience.
CLIMATE CHANGE AND DISASTER
47. Leaders reaffirmed that climate change remains the single greatest threat to the security,
livelihoods and wellbeing of Pacific peoples and underscored the urgency of ambitious global
action consistent with the best available science. Leaders endorsed the continued implementation

10
of the COP31 Pacific Partnership and the work of the Pacific COP31 Envoys to advance regional
priorities across the strategic pillars of keeping 1.5°C within reach, enhancing access to climate
finance, elevating the ocean-climate nexus, and amplifying Pacific voices and leadership. Leaders
further noted the importance of a focused, high-level declaration from the Pacific Pre-COP31
Leaders Meeting to build political momentum towards COP31.
48. Leaders recognised the unique opportunity presented by hosting of the Pacific Pre-COP
31 meetings by Fiji, Tuvalu, and Australia to recognise the special circumstances of Small Island
Developing States (SIDS) and Least Developed Countries, and to reaffirm the region’s collective
resolve for ambitious climate action.
49. Leaders welcomed and endorsed the BELAU Declaration for Urgent and Inclusive
Climate Action to Keep 1.5°C Within Reach, reaffirming their collective commitment to keeping
the 1.5°C Paris Agreement temperature goal within reach and strengthening international action
on climate change. Through the Declaration, Leaders reaffirmed the importance of urgent climate
action, greater recognition of the ocean-climate nexus, accelerating the transition away from fossil
fuels towards a renewable energy future, and improving access to climate finance for Pacific Island
countries. In line with this, Leaders stressed the need to prioritise climate mitigation and
recognised the region’s ocean and forests are essential global carbon sinks. Leaders noted
Tuvalu’s hosting of the 2nd International Conference on Transitioning Away from Fossil Fuels in
April 2027.
50. Leaders affirmed the leadership of Pacific women in driving climate action and endorsed
the Pacific Pact on Gender and Climate Action as the region’s public political commitment to lead
and accelerate implementation of the Belém Gender Action Plan in the Pacific. Leaders further
endorsed the Pact as a commitment for delivering gender-responsive climate action, financing and
implementation across the region.
51. Leaders continued to recognise the Green Climate Fund as a key climate partner and called
for an adequate and timely third replenishment and improved SIDS access to climate finance,
alongside strengthened engagement with regional financing mechanisms, including the PRF, to
meet the region's climate needs.
52. Leaders discussed the opportunity to accelerate the region's renewable energy transition
through strengthened regional coordination and practical investment partnerships and called for
increased investment in renewable energy across the Blue Pacific. To strengthen regional
leadership and implementation, Leaders approved the Terms of Reference for the Renewable
Energy Commissioner and supported the placement of the role within the SPC, with dual
reporting arrangements to SPC and the Pacific Islands Forum Secretariat (PIFS) reflecting its
regional mandate.
53. Leaders also commended Vanuatu for its leadership in securing the adoption of the historic
United Nations General Assembly Resolution welcoming the International Court of Justice
Advisory Opinion on the Obligations of States in Respect of Climate Change and encouraging
States to operationalise it. Leaders also reaffirmed the importance of continued collective
advocacy to protect the rights, interests, sovereignty, and future of Pacific peoples in the face of
climate change and sea-level rise.
54. Leaders noted the increased likelihood of a strong El Niño event and underscored the
importance of strengthening regional preparedness and resilience, particularly in relation to water

11
security, drought and other extreme weather impacts. Leaders recognised the value of Pacific-
owned initiatives, including the PRF and Weather Ready Pacific, in enhancing forecasting, early
warning systems and anticipatory action, and reaffirmed the importance of a comprehensive
multi-hazard approach to resilience and disaster risk management. In this regard, Leaders
recognised that geohazards, including earthquakes, volcanic activity and tsunamis, pose
significant risks across the Blue Pacific and called for their strengthened integration within
regional early warning systems, preparedness frameworks, and resilience financing arrangements.
55. Leaders noted the deployment of the Pacific Climate Mobility Initiative to support
Members in their national efforts to implement the Pacific Regional Framework for Climate
Mobility, through delivering knowledge and data to inform national policies and planning for
translating regional commitments into practical national action, and enhanced readiness to access
international climate finance to address climate mobility.
OCEAN AND ENVIRONMENT
56. Leaders reaffirmed that the ocean remains central to the sovereignty, security, prosperity,
culture, and identity of Pacific peoples. Leaders endorsed the 2026 Pacific Islands Forum
Leaders’ Ocean Statement to frame the region’s advocacy and action on Pacific Ocean priorities
and reaffirmed the importance of coherent and coordinated regional engagement across
international processes relating to oceans, climate change, biodiversity, fisheries, pollution and
sustainable development. Leaders further tasked the OPOC to convene regional preparations
ahead of the first Conference of the Parties to the Agreement on Marine Biological Diversity of
Areas beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ Agreement) in January 2027 and encouraged
continued mobilisation of resources to support ratification and implementation of the Agreement
across the region.
57. Leaders reaffirmed the 2021 Declaration on Preserving Maritime Zones in the face of
Climate Change-related Sea-Level Rise and the 2023 Declaration on the Continuity of Statehood
and the Protection of Persons Affected by Climate Change-related Sea-Level Rise, and further
reaffirmed the importance of safeguarding the sovereign rights and jurisdictions of Members in
the face of sea-level rise. Leaders committed to urgently conclude outstanding maritime
boundaries delimitation treaties, bring maritime boundary agreements into force, and deposit
relevant coordinates and charts with the United Nations, consistent with international law and
previous Forum Leaders’ decisions.
58. Leaders reaffirmed the critical importance of fisheries and aquaculture to the prosperity,
food security, livelihoods, culture, economies and ocean stewardship of the Blue Pacific. Leaders
endorsed the Vakamanu Regional Pacific Fisheries Strategy 2026-2035 as the region’s
overarching fisheries and aquaculture framework and underscored the importance of sustainable
management, protection of Members’ sovereign rights, climate resilience, and securing greater
economic and social benefits from fisheries resources for Pacific peoples.
59. Recognising the importance of informed and inclusive regional dialogue on deep sea
minerals, Leaders endorsed the recommendations contained in the Implementation Plan for
Member-Led Regional Approach to Deep Sea Minerals and the Paper on Regional Repository for
Deep Sea Mineral Data and Knowledge. Leaders reaffirmed that regional cooperation on deep
sea minerals should continue to be guided by Member leadership, without prejudice to the
sovereign rights of Members over natural resources within their national jurisdiction, equitable

12
access to science, data and knowledge, and adherence to international law, including the United
Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.
60. Leaders noted the offer by Vanuatu of its Global Scientific Assessment of Deep-Sea
Ecosystems as an independent scientific contribution that can support the Pacific Deep-Sea
Knowledge Partnership and the wider implementation of the regional approach.
61. Leaders reaffirmed the need for an ambitious comprehensive and legally binding global
agreement that addresses the full life cycle of plastics from production to waste, including existing
plastic pollution in the marine environment, and reiterated the region’s commitment to preventing
marine litter and plastic pollution, and protecting the health of the Pacific Ocean and its peoples.
62. Leaders reaffirmed the 2021 Pacific Islands Forum Declaration on the Prevention of
Marine Litter and Plastic Pollution and called for collaboration on a Regional Extended Producer
Responsibility Framework tailored to Pacific import-dependent economies.
63. Leaders reaffirmed their collective commitment to the East New Britain Initiative,
recognising its pivotal role as a regional hub for tuna processing and services that enhances our
sovereignty over marine resources. Leaders emphasised the necessity of increasing the
meaningful participation of SIDS across the entire tuna value chain. Leaders further called for
the urgent strengthening of management measures on the high seas to eliminate illegal, unreported,
and unregulated (IUU) fishing, while demanding that such measures are designed and
implemented in a manner that avoids transferring a disproportionate conservation or administrative
burden onto SIDS, thereby securing a sustainable and equitable blue economy for the region.
64. Leaders acknowledged the signing of the Pacific Blue Shipping Partnership (PBSP)
Charter on 12 June 2026 by Fiji, the Republic of the Marshall Islands, the Federated States of
Micronesia, Kiribati, Naoero, Tuvalu, and Vanuatu, and the subsequent signing by Tonga, and the
convening of the inaugural PBSP Ministerial Council meeting, which formally committed to a
coordinated approach to shipping decarbonisation through the Green Climate Fund (GCF).
65. Leaders acknowledged the Kingdom of Tonga’s concern regarding the protection of

Preamble
1. Reaffirming that climate change remains the single greatest threat to the security, livelihoods,
wellbeing and future of the peoples of the Pacific,
2. Reaffirming that as a Blue Pacific Continent, we stand united in confronting an existential crisis
that is already causing severe and irreversible impacts across our region, including loss and
damage, displacement, ecosystem degradation, threats to food and water security, and growing
economic and fiscal pressures,
3. Recognising the impacts on our region of fossil fuel dependency and the current fossil fuel crisis
threatening our energy security,
4. Recalling the 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent, Kainaki II Declaration for Urgent
Climate Change Action Now, the Boe Declaration, the Blue Pacific Ocean of Peace Declaration,
and other regional commitments, that urgent climate action is fundamental to Pacific survival,
resilience, security, sustainable development and prosperity,
5. Reaffirming our unwavering commitment to keep 1.5°C within reach in accordance with
the Paris Agreement, and underscoring our strong concern that current global actions and the
aggregate ambition and implementation of NDCs are insufficient to halt a 1.5°C overshoot,
6. Recognising and reaffirming the historic Advisory Opinions of the International Tribunal for
the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) relating to the protection of the marine environment in relation to
Climate Change and the International Court of Justice (ICJ) on the Obligations of States in
Respect of Climate Change as landmark opinions on States’ responsibilities under international
law, providing powerful contributions to global efforts to protect the climate system and
safeguard present and future generations,
7. Recognising the United Nations General Assembly Resolution which welcomed the unanimous
ICJ Advisory Opinion and affirmed its importance as an authoritative contribution to the
clarification of existing international law,
8. Reiterating that Pacific peoples contribute least to global emissions yet bear among the gravest
impacts of climate change,
9. Acknowledging the disproportionate impacts of climate change on women and girls and
reaffirming our commitment to gender-responsive climate action,
We, the Leaders of the Pacific Island Forum, therefore:
10. Recognise that Pacific leadership must be demonstrated through action and thereby commit to:
i. submitting and implementing ambitious NDCs and National Adaptation Plans in this
critical decade to keep the 1.5°C pathway within reach,

16
ii. affirm our responsibility and leadership in protecting the Blue Pacific Continent,
recognising the vast and shared ocean spaces held through our collective Exclusive
Economic zones,
iii. accelerate development of a Regional Renewable Energy Transition Strategy and
implementation of the renewed Framework for Energy Security and Resilience in the
Pacific (FESRIP 2.0), to accelerate transition away from fossil fuels to progress towards a
100 percent renewable energy future for the Blue Pacific, including with support from the
new Pacific Renewable Energy Commissioner once established, recognising differing
national circumstances and pathways.
iv. elevating the importance of improving access to finance at COP31 and preparing the
Access to Finance Declaration and Action Plan for SIDS and Least Developed Countries,
v. implementing the Pacific Pact on Gender and Climate Action in line with the Belem
Gender Action Plan,
vi. strengthening regional monitoring, reporting and existing accountability mechanisms for
climate commitments, with the support of relevant CROP agencies,
vii. amplifying Pacific leadership and voices at the Pacific Pre-COP leaders’ events in Fiji and

to climate finance, and elevating the ocean-climate nexus, Pacific youth leadership and
intergenerational equity in climate decision-making,
11. Call upon the International Community for urgent climate action now across the following
areas:
Urgent action to Keep 1.5°C Within Reach
12. Reaffirm our call on all countries to pursue efforts and implement measures to hold the increase
in global average temperatures to 1.5°C degrees in accordance with the Paris Agreement, taking
into account their different national circumstances and approaches, including by:
i. submitting and implementing ambitious NDCs aligned with limiting global warming to
1.5°C
ii. supporting adequate means of implementation, including through regional and multilateral
programs, for Pacific Islands Forum Members to submit and deliver their NDCs;
iii. affirming unequivocally that the best available science, as reflected in the IPCC's work and
enriched by Pacific Indigenous and Traditional Knowledge, must continue to guide global
climate action, including as a critical input to the second Global Stocktake;
iv. accelerating the transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems in a just, orderly and
equitable manner, accelerating action in this critical decade, so as to achieve net-zero
greenhouse gas emissions by 2050 consistent with 1.5°C aligned pathways.
v. taking collective action to reduce near-term warming by addressing short-lived climate
pollutants, including through strengthened action to reduce global methane emissions,
vi. giving effect to States’ respective obligations under international law to ensure the
protection of the climate system and other parts of the environment from anthropogenic
greenhouse gas emissions, as identified by the Court in the ICJ Advisory Opinion,
vii. encouraging all States to act consistently with UN General Assembly Resolution 80/263 of
20 May 2026 in implementing their respective obligations nationally and internationally.

with their respective international obligations, noting the Court’s finding in its advisory

v. scaled-up finance to avert, minimise and address loss and damage, including through

Damage and other relevant financing arrangements.
vi. scaled up, predictable finance, including grant-based finance that is directly accessible to
Pacific SIDS to support renewable energy transition and adaptation without

vii. the international community and all multilateral and bilateral partners to provide financing
to operationalise a fully capitalised Pacific Resilience Facility as the cornerstone of our
financial architecture to build resilience through Pacific community-led and Pacific
community-owned interventions on scale.

PACIFIC PACT ON GENDER AND CLIMATE ACTION
1. We, the Leaders of the Pacific Islands Forum, reaffirm our commitment to leading the effective
implementation of the Belém Gender Action Plan, consistent with national priorities, capacities and
circumstances, and recognising the diverse needs of Forum Island Members, as reflected in our
Nationally Determined Contributions and National Adaptation Plans.
2. We resolve to strengthen regional cooperation through three complementary areas of action:
i. promoting Pacific women's leadership in climate decision-making;
ii. strengthening gender-responsive climate data and evidence; and
iii. advancing gender-responsive approaches to climate finance.
3. We reaffirm that our climate leadership is inseparable from our stewardship of the Blue Pacific
Continent. We recognise the vital leadership, knowledge, agency and contributions of Pacific women
in sustaining resilient communities, healthy oceans and sustainable livelihoods. Pacific women are
acknowledged as custodians of traditional and Indigenous knowledge systems, customary marine
tenure and resource management practices, and play critical roles across ocean-based economies, food
systems and community resilience.
Pacific women’s leadership in climate decisions
4. We commit to advance women’s leadership and voice in climate action at every level. We will
strengthen women’s representation in our national climate delegations, ministerial portfolios and
climate governance bodies, and we will sustain a network of Pacific women climate negotiators with
continuity across negotiations.
5. We will place women in the governance of how our climate responses are designed and
delivered, in our national climate finance entities, and in our engagement with the Fund for Responding
to Loss and Damage and other climate funds. We will carry Pacific women’s leadership into the ocean–
climate forums where our future is decided.
6. We recognise Pacific women’s traditional knowledge and leadership as climate leadership in
their own right. In our communities, women lead the everyday work where much of our adaptation
actually happens, and they hold deep knowledge of our seasons, waters, marine and food systems. We
commit to value this knowledge and leadership alongside formal qualifications and titles, and to ensure
it shapes the climate decisions taken in our countries and our region.
Building Pacific evidence on women’s resilience
7. We commit to build the gender and climate evidence grounded in Pacific realities to strengthen
our gender appropriate climate response, while recognising existing data constraints and capacity gaps.
8. We will advance implementation of our Nationally Determined Contributions and National
Adaptation Plans, while strengthening the wider ecosystem of Pacific gender responsive climate action
through regional cooperation, community-led initiatives and national development efforts that build
resilience and support a climate-resilient future.
9. We will develop Gender Country Profiles with a climate and disaster lens, refreshed on our
own national timelines, and we will build indicators grounded in what is measurable and meaningful
in our contexts — including the realities global data systems miss: women’s adaptation work,

20
traditional ocean knowledge, customary marine tenure, and gendered ocean livelihoods such as reef
gleaning, near-shore fishing, post-harvest processing and coastal cultural economies.
Making climate finance reach women
10. We commit to ensuring that climate finance contributes to equitable and inclusive outcomes
and supports the resilience of communities, particularly for women, girls and those most vulnerable to
climate change.
11. We will press for climate finance to reach communities and civil society organisations directly,
including women-led organisations and women’s community groups who carry much of the everyday
work of resilience and reach women in places no national mechanism does.
12. We will commit to tracking gender outcomes reported and verified across the multilateral
Climate Funds, bilateral funding, private sector and other sources of climate financing.
13. We will direct attention to where Pacific women’s livelihoods, food security and knowledge
are most concentrated — coastal and reef-dependent communities — in how adaptation finance is
accessed and used.
Our Call
14. We, Pacific Leaders, call on our partners, on the climate funds, and on the international
community gathering at pre-COP31 and COP31 to recognise this Pact and the PRF as the Pacific’s
own contribution to gender-responsive climate action, and to align their support with what we, the
Pacific, commit to lead.

Preamble
