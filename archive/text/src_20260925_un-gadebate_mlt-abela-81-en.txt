PRESS RELEASE BY THE OFFICE OF THE PRIME MINISTER Speech by
Prime Minister Robert Abela at the 81st session of the United Nations
General Assembly

Mr President,
Excellencies,
Distinguished delegates.

The chosen theme for this years General Assembly is ‘Restoring Trust, Managing Transformation:
I dont think there could be a more appropriate framing for our discussions this week
Trust, or more properly the lack thereof, is the greatest challenge facing our world

The erosion of trust that has gripped all our societies runs deep. Where once people embraced trust, now
people are gripped by suspicion. Suspicion of governments. Suspicion of institutions. Suspicion of businesses.
Even, all too often, suspicion of each other.

lam talking about suspicion, not scepticism.

Scepticism, the legitimate questioning of those who hold power over peoples lives, is the lifeblood of any
democratic society. Suspicion, the assumption that others are automatically looking out for their interests and
not those of the common good, is poison to any democratic society,

No community can succeed if we blindly believe that our neighbours are out to do us down. Just as no country
can succeed if it blindly believes that its neighbours, even those it may long have seen as friends, are out to get
it

Which means restoring trust, rebuilding alliances, remaking friendships, must be the central mission for us all. At
home and abroad. It must also be the central mission of the remaking of this United Nations. To change, you
have to believe.

We know we need to change how the UN works, how it takes decisions, and how it delivers on the ground, But,
we can only make that change, achieve that transformation, if we believe throughout that change is possible.

Nobody ever successfully renovated a house without the faith that, however hard the work, however many
setbacks faced on the way, the end result would be a real improvement on what went before.

So, we need to show faith that, however hard the work, however many setbacks faced on the way, the end result
of transforming this house, this home of all the nations will be a real step forward.

And as this United Nations embarks on its ninth decade of service, the need for a step change in how the
international community works together, solves problems together, is arguably needed more acutely now than
ever before.

That erosion of trust is leading to the corrosion of the international order.

Not the improvement of the international order, but the destruction of the international order. An international
order that for all its flaws and failures has seen more children escape hunger,
more families live in peace, and more children grow into old age than any era before it.

To destroy the international settlement, without any idea of what comes next, would be an act of wonton
destruction that would, rightly, never be forgiven by the tens of millions whose futures it would betray.

So let us say it loud and clear. Let us be utterly unequivocalNow, more than ever, is a moment for more co-
operation, not less; more dialogue, not less; more trust, not less.

That is the only way we will find paths to progress on the all too many, all too intractable conflicts blighting our
world. In Malta we have always been a bridge between Europe and the Middle East.

So, let me focus on two of those conflicts on our doorstep,

Firstly, Ukraine

It is all too easy, and all too great a mistake, for us to fall in the trap that repelling the invasion of Ukraine
becomes another never-ending conflict. A grinding tragedy on the corner of our continent, about which we can
do nothing

Malta will never accept that despair. Europe must never succumb to that despair. The world must never turn its
head away in despair.

Malta remains as adamant in our condemnation of the invasion today, as we were on day one in February 2022
And Malta is equally as adamant that a just and lasting peace must guarantee Ukraine's sovereignty within its
internationally recognised borders. That will only come about through a diplomatic process the Ukrainian people
can have full trust in

They will only have such trust if the process follows a full and unconditional ceasefire, and the full participation of
Ukraine in the subsequent peace talks

Rightly, the Ukrainian people will never have trust in any search for a settlement which involves their government
being frog-marched to the table, one arm tied behind its back, whilst the guns still blaze.

The issue of trust is, clearly, also central to the situation in Gaza.

The people of Israel need to have trust that Hamas will be disarmed and play no future role in the governance or
security structures of Gaza. But the people of Gaza need to have trust that there is a path to a just peace. It is
impossible to build trust with a humanitarian situation as acute as that being experienced in Gaza right now.

Malta's voice remains, unwaveringly, with the global calls for safe, sustained and unhindered humanitarian
access throughout Gaza, in accordance with international law. We firmly believe the Gaza Peace Plan provides
the best path towards ending the conflict, and advancing credible political progress.

To achieve that progress, all parties must fully respect and implement the objectives and commitments set out
in the planand naturally, no progress can be made if there is any question of the forced displacement of any of
the Palestinian population.

Instead, it is incumbent on all of us in the international community to rebuild the trust needed to underpin the
conditions where genuine progress can be made towards a negotiated and lasting Two-State solution

Nobody is under any illusion how out of reach that seems at this moment. But nor is this the moment to shy away

Sadly, any progress towards a long-term solution is being endangered by the situation in the West Bank.
Settlement expansion, running directly counter to the resolutions of this General Assembly and designed to

So too must the violent settler attacks on Palestinian communities, increasing at an alarming rate, be halted.
These are essential pre-conditions to a lasting peace

As is a credible and reformed Palestinian Authority that can demonstrate to the citizens of both Palestine and
Israel that it has the transparency, accountability, institutional effectiveness, and above all the democratic
legitimacy to be a truly effective partner for peace.

Malta stands ready to play its part in building the capacity of both a renewed, democratic Palestinian Authority
and the robust civil society that must underpin it. Not just in Ukraine, Gaza and Israel but across the world, be it
the conflict in Sudan, the ongoing violence in Myanmar, or instability across the Sahel, it is evident that
diminished trust means diminished chances of finding solutions

You could put it as starkly as, no trust, equals no peace.

Of course, no matter the conflict. each with its own circumstances, there is no easy way of building trust. But
equally, there is no easy way of avoiding the need to build trust.

We all know, from our own lives, as much as from our own countries, just how hard it is to rebuild trust when it
has evaporated. Yet, we also know the consequences if we give up trying and the rewards that can come if,
however slowly and however tentatively, we can start,

If it is hard enough to build trust human to human, face to face, it can be infinitely harder to build trust of
machines, of the technology shaping all our lives. Yet, the challenge of how we can trust that technology, and the
organisations behind it, is rapidly becoming the most pressing of our age.

The sheer speed and sheer power of Artificial Intelligence is mind-blowing. It appears to be moving far too fast
for anybody, let alone governments, to keep pace.

It can seem, whether from the perspective of leader, consumer or parent, to be overwhelming

There is no escaping that this is the biggest change. and the biggest challenge. any of us will experience in our
lifetime. So much so, that we barely have the words to describe quite how monumental. disruption itis.

The usual phrases, step-change:; paradigm shift, just don't seem to capture the scale. And we should also be
under no illusion that the existing rules, the traditional models of governance are, in any way, up to grappling
with the revolution we are living through.

Which is why, if we stand any chance of helping to build citizens’ trust in advanced technologies. we need to
begin by being honest, that governments, just as individuals, are finding our way.

That we don't have all the answers
What we do have, what we must have, is the will to act, and the right values to underpin those actions, And, of
course, it almost goes without saying that no state, no matter how micro or how mighty, has any chance of

getting a grip if they try to go it alone.

Tackling them must be a priority, the priority for this United Nations,

That is why the goal of this General Assembly, ‘A United Nations that Delivers for All’ is so important and so

If we are to have any hope of renewing the trust of the citizens we serve in global institutions and the global

We must never lose sight and never lose faith in the values that brought us together in the first place, as nations

Malta is, and always will be, a passionate advocate of and passionately committed to multilateralism. At the core

implementation of those values, the way we turn those fine words into fine actions, must always be a process of

We can never stand still if we are to keep cooperation alive
So, Malta remains thoroughly committed to the implementation of the Pact for the Future and all its associated
actions. Reforms must remain member state driven, and firmly anchored in the inter-governmental process. And

efficiency must not come at the expense of effectiveness on the ground.

Malta solidly backs a strong and effective Resident Coordinator system, as the key anchor of UN action at a

To do nothing, to stand by and wash our hands, would be a moral and practical betrayal of the citizens who put
their faith in us that we won't flinch from the biggest problems and the toughest solutions

So, we must not stand by, We must stand firm. Stand firm against the torrent of disinformation that fuels the
deniers Stand firm against the vested interests that seek to block action. Stand firm against the naysayers who
say that because all dontt choose to act, nobody should

To those cynics, | want to point you to an instance that shows hope can triumph over defeatism.

In 1967, representing the then geographically smallest member state of the UN, a member of the UN for less
than three years, Arvid Pardo spoke up in this General Assembly.In a stirring speech, Malta's Permanent
Representative made a passionate plea to the world’s common humanity.

He may have been speaking on behalf of a small nation, but he had an ambitious vision

Pardo called for the world to treat the resources of the oceans beyond their own borders, as the “common
heritage of mankind”

His demand for a law of the sea was prescient. but to achieve it took persistence. In fact. it took 27 years before
UNCLOS came into being.

What motivated Malta's action then, is what motivates Malta’s demands now. Doing nothing meant leaving the
world's oceans to the mercy of the world's biggest, strongest, richest nations

Taking action showed that, and | quote, ‘they belonged to all of humanity, those living and those who still had to
be born?

That spirit was evident when in 1988, right at the start of the concern about global warming, Malta proposed the
UN General Assembly resolution that recognised climate change as a global problem requiring collective, global

solutions.

Suspicion of collective action wont be counteracted by retreat, it will only be overcome by the strength to turn
convictions into action.

initial aspirations into sustainable attainment, takes something more than just dreams

It takes trust.
Trust that by working together, we achieve more than we could ever achieve alone.
