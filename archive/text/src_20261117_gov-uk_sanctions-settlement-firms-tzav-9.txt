Foreign Secretary implements first sanctions package since the September reset of the UK’s approach to Israeli actions in the West Bank and the expansion of the Global Human Rights sanctions regime
Today’s sanctions target construction and property development firms involved in settlement expansion, and an activist group notorious for blocking aid entering Gaza
The UK is stepping up efforts to tackle threats to the two-state solution, and demonstrating the UK will not stand by as the humanitarian crisis deepens in Gaza
The UK has imposed new sanctions on companies involved in the expansion of illegal settlements in the West Bank and an activist group responsible for blocking aid to Gaza.
As part of the UK’s leadership on protecting the viability of a two-state solution, the UK has taken firm action against eight construction and property development firms which will disrupt their flows of finance and ability to support further construction in the West Bank.
These measures deliver on the Foreign Secretary’s pledge on 8 September to respond to the accelerating rate of settlement expansion in the West Bank, which risks rendering the two-state solution unviable.
Made possible by the UK’s expansion of its Global Human Rights sanctions regime, this announcement comes just before the final deadlines for the E1 tender, a project which threatens the viability of lasting peace for Israelis and Palestinians.
This package has been announced in response to the ongoing situation of accelerated settlement expansion, which the Government of Israel has not only refused to clamp down on, but has facilitated and encouraged. More settlements have been approved under the four years of this Israeli government than in the previous twenty years. The UK welcomes and encourages trade with green line Israel and firmly distinguishes this with activity in settlements.
Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said:
Protecting the two-state solution is the North Star of this Government’s approach.
That is why my message on E1 is clear: do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from the destruction of the two-state solution. And we will not stand by while those blocking desperately needed aid deepen the intolerable suffering of civilians in Gaza.
The status quo in the West Bank and Gaza is unacceptable. We are committed to working productively with a new Israeli government and the Palestinian Authority to advance lasting peace and security for Israelis and Palestinians alike.
The Foreign Secretary is also announcing new sanctions targeting the blocking of aid deliveries to Gaza. Today’s measures target Tzav 9, an Israeli activist movement, which has organised and carried out blockades to prevent humanitarian aid convoys entering Gaza as recently as October.
Gazan civilians are in desperate need of winter supplies, with catastrophic floods leaving nearly 4000 households left without shelter and 1.4 million people living in tents or makeshift shelters.
According to the UN, over 150,000 tents and 300,000 tarpaulins that humanitarian partners and donors purchased are outside Gaza ready to go in but are being held by the Israeli authorities. The Israeli authorities also continue to block timber from entering, leaving no emergency shelter kits available in Gaza.
Today’s action follows action this week by the UK to provide additional humanitarian support in Gaza and pressure the Israeli government to allow more aid in. Last week (12 November), Minister Doughty announced that the UK will release a further £5 million to UNICEF’s winter programming following the devastating floods and the UK convened more than 50 partners at the UN to call on the Israeli authorities to let more aid in.
Notes to editors
The UK is clear that settlements are a flagrant breach of international law. Settlement expansion and attempts to create irreversible divisions on the ground threaten peace, security and the prospects for a viable Palestinian state. A two-state solution remains the only way to ensure long-term peace and security for both Palestinians and Israelis. The UK will continue to act in pursuit of this goal.
The entities sanctioned today are construction, property development and architectural businesses that have helped to plan, build, supply or market housing and public infrastructure in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank. We are also sanctioning a group which has blocked aid getting into Gaza.
These designations have been made under the UK’s expanded Global Human Rights and International Humanitarian Law sanctions regime. The legislation was laid on 13 October and expanded the scope of the UK’s existing Global Human Rights regime to enable the UK to designate individuals and companies involved in serious violations of international humanitarian law anywhere in the world.
As the Foreign Secretary announced in September, the FCDO is also developing separately legislation for sanctions banning the import of settlement goods and the provision of advertising services for settlement properties in the UK.
Endangering the viability of a future Palestinian state, the E1 project would cut across the heart of Palestine and risks separating the West Bank from East Jerusalem. The UK and the wider international community has repeatedly called on the Israeli government to halt the plans immediately, retract the tender and stop all illegal settlement expansion.
Minister Doughty announced in the House on 12 November that the UK will provide £5 million in humanitarian support to UNICEF, focused on the winter response including the impact of flooding over the weekend. This is in addition to £14 million we were already providing to UNICEF this year to support nutrition, child protection, health, water and sanitation and preparation for winter. This is part of our £88m allocation for humanitarian and early recovery support this financial year.
The UK brought together more than 50 countries on 12 November to call on Israel to lift restrictions and allow significantly more aid into Gaza. This follows the UK-led Call to Action agreed with international partners at the UN General Assembly in September and continued UK diplomatic pressure.
Entities sanctioned today
Har Hevron Development Company Ltd - a development company which carries out construction and infrastructure works for, and supplies housing units to, Israeli settlements and outposts in the southern West Bank. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Hemdat Shomron Ltd - a property developer and building contractor which initiates, plans, builds and markets housing in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including at Einav, Kfar Tapuach, Pedu’el, Ma’ale Efrayim, Mitzpe Yericho and Dolev. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
S.A.G. (Velvel) Construction and Development Ltd - an Israeli contractor and developer which builds and markets housing and public buildings in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including at Efrat, Shilo and Eli. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Lev HaHar Yazamut Ltd - an Israeli property developer which builds and markets housing in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including at Itamar, Elon Moreh, Bruchin, Rechelim, and Neria. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Harey Zahav - an Israeli property developer which initiates, plans, builds and markets housing in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank and which markets that housing to purchasers in Israel and abroad. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Ariel Residential Judea & Samaria Ltd - an Israeli property developer which initiates, plans, builds and markets housing in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including at Ariel, Alei Zahav, Pedu’el, Metzad, and Efrat. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Ami Gvirtzman Architects 2021 Ltd - an Israeli architectural and planning practice which designs housing, neighbourhoods and public infrastructure for Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including at Carmel, Ma’ale Amos, Tekoa and Efrat. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Z.F. Building Company Ltd - an Israeli property developer which initiates, plans, builds and markets housing in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including at Ma’ale Yisrael, Nokdim and Kiryat Netafim. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
Tzav 9 – an Israeli activist movement whose stated mission has been to stop humanitarian aid entering the Gaza Strip, and which has organised and carried out blockades of crossings, ports and roads used by humanitarian aid convoys bound for Gaza. They are now subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.
