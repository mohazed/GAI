JOINT STATEMENT
BETWEEN MALAYSIA AND THE ARAB REPUBLIC OF EGYPT ON THE
OCCASION OF THE STATE VISIT BY HIS EXCELLENCY ABDEL FATTAH AL SISI,
PRESIDENT OF THE ARAB REPUBLIC OF EGYPT TO MALAYSIA
9-10 OCTOBER 2026
1. At the invitation of His Majesty Sultan Ibrahim, King of Malaysia, His Excellency Abdel
Fattah Al Sisi, President of the Arab Republic of Egypt undertook a State Visit to
Malaysia from 9 to 10 October 2026. This marked President Al Sisi’s first State Visit to
Malaysia.
2. During the visit, President Al Sisi was accorded a State Welcoming Ceremony,
followed by an Audience with His Majesty Sultan Ibrahim at the Istana Negara.
Honourable Dato’ Seri Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister of Malaysia and President Al Sisi,
subsequently held a bilateral meeting on 10 October 2026, where both Leaders engaged
in extensive and in-depth discussions, covering the full spectrum of bilateral relations.
This encompasses political engagement, trade and investment, defence and security
cooperation, digital economy, energy, agriculture, health, education, tourism, people-to-
people contacts as well as regional and international issues of mutual concern.
3. Since the establishment of diplomatic relations on 10 November 1959, Malaysia and
Egypt have enjoyed a strong and enduring partnership underpinned by mutual respect,
shared values and common aspirations. While tracing back the historical ties between
the two countries, the bilateral relations have witnessed profound growth and have
continuously strengthened throughout the decades.
4. Recalling the commitment made on 10 November 2024 in Cairo, Prime Minister
Anwar Ibrahim and President Al Sisi announced the elevation of bilateral relations
between Malaysia and Egypt to a Strategic Partnership. Under this framework, both
Leaders reaffirmed their shared interest to accelerate efforts in building a more dynamic
and resilient partnership across all sectors, thereby jointly promoting global prosperity
and stability. Both Leaders acknowledged the importance of regular dialogue through
existing bilateral institutional mechanisms and in this regard, agreed to convene the
Second Joint Commission Meeting (JCM) and the Second Bilateral Consultations (BC)
at the earliest opportunity.
5. Both Leaders lauded the growing economic partnership between Malaysia and Egypt,
noting the vast untapped economic potential between the two nations. In this spirit, both
Leaders agreed to enhance trade and investment, encouraging business-led
cooperation in areas such as agriculture; the halal industry; education; Technical and
Vocational Education and Training (TVET); digital transformation; renewable energy;
infrastructure development; automotive; manufacturing and semiconductors.
Furthermore, both Leaders recognised that their countries are strategically positioned
to serve as economic hubs and gateways for one another into the broader ASEAN, Arab,
African and European markets.

6. Both Leaders recognised the longstanding and cordial defence and security relations
between the two countries, built upon mutually beneficial cooperation and the
importance of enhancing these relations.
7. Both Leaders acknowledged the immense benefits of people-to-people exchanges in
forging closer bonds of friendship between the two nations. These include the exchange
of scholarships, human capital development and academic cooperation. Malaysia
remains committed to strengthening capacity-building cooperation with Egypt, in areas
of mutually agreed priorities, including through the Malaysian Technical Cooperation
Programme (MTCP).
8. Both Leaders underscored the importance of strengthening cooperation and the long-
standing partnership in education, higher education and capacity building across all
fields of religious and Arabic studies, as well as technical and scientific programs. The
two countries will continue to expand these academic ties through collaborative
research, language programs, and student exchange initiatives that strengthen cultural,
religious, and scientific cooperation.
9. Both Leaders emphasised the importance of air connectivity in supporting closer
economic cooperation and encouraged their respective national airlines to explore
opportunities for enhanced collaboration. Recognising its significance for tourism, trade,
and cultural exchanges, the Leaders reaffirmed their shared commitment to facilitating
greater two-way travel and strengthening people-to-people connections.
10. On ASEAN, Malaysia welcomed Egypt’s accession to the Treaty of Amity and
Cooperation in Southeast Asia (TAC) in 2016 and expressed support for Egypt’s
proposal to formalise its engagement with ASEAN by pursuing Sectoral Dialogue
Partner status. Malaysia believes this cooperation will further solidify Egypt's relations
with all ASEAN Member States. Malaysia looks forward to Egypt’s support for ASEAN
to explore cooperation with the African Union (AU) as ASEAN continues to diversify and
strengthen its resilience through expanding its relations with regions across the world.
11. The two countries expressed their commitment to working together towards
achieving peace and stability in the Gulf region and the West Asia, including in the Strait
of Hormuz, and underscored the importance of ensuring freedom of navigation,
according to international law.
12. The two Leaders reaffirmed that the Palestinian issue remains the core issue in
West Asia and the Middle East, and stressed the importance of achieving a just and
lasting peace and the implementation of the two-state solution, which guarantees the
establishment of an independent and sovereign Palestinian state based on the pre-1967
borders with East Jerusalem as its capital. They renewed their categorical rejection of
any plans to displace the Palestinian people from their homeland under any pretexts or
labels. The two sides called for the need to consolidate the ceasefire in the Gaza Strip,
facilitate the flow of humanitarian aid without restrictions, and ensure the full and
effective implementation of United Nations Security Council Resolution 2803 (2025), as
well as the return of the Palestinian Authority to the Strip at the soonest time. Prime
Minister Anwar Ibrahim expressed Malaysia’s deep appreciation for Egypt’s pivotal role

in working to end the war in the Gaza Strip and facilitating the delivery of Malaysian
humanitarian assistance to the Palestinian people in the Strip. They expressed their
concern about the dangerous escalation in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, and
condemned the increased pace of settler violence, land confiscation, and the
construction of settlements. They stressed the importance of preserving the unity of the
Palestinian territories, supporting the role of the United Nations Relief and Works
Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East, and the need to preserve and uphold
its mandate.
13. The two Leaders affirmed the position of Malaysia and Egypt in support of the
sovereignty, unity, and territorial integrity of Lebanon, and stressed the need for an end
to all occupying power attacks and for its withdrawal from the occupied Lebanese
territories, as well as the full and non-selective implementation of United Nations
Security Council Resolution 1701 (2006). They also reiterated their condemnation of
violations by the occupying power and incursions into Syrian territory, calling for the
withdrawal from the occupied Syrian territories and compliance with the 1974
Disengagement of Forces Agreement.
14. Both Leaders affirmed the critical importance of maintaining peace, security and
stability in the Horn of Africa and Sudan. They underscored the necessity of respecting
the sovereignty and territorial integrity of all states in the region, urging all parties to
refrain from taking any unilateral measures or arrangements that allow access to the red
sea and the Gulf of Aden in violation of the sovereign rights of coastal countries. To this
end, they stressed that the governance and security of the red sea are the exclusive
responsibility of its coastal countries, and they also underscored the importance of the
freedom of navigation and unimpeded international trade in accordance with
international law, including the 1982 United Nations Convention of the Law of the Sea
(UNCLOS), to address common challenges and prevent the escalation of tensions.
15. The Malaysian side recognised the importance of the Nile River to Egypt's water
security. Both sides emphasised the importance of peaceful resolution of disputes, and
refrain from taking unilateral actions. They urged all Nile Basin countries to strengthen
dialogue to achieve mutual benefit and consensus in accordance with international law.
16. Both Leaders reaffirmed their commitment to active cooperation across various
multilateral platforms, particularly the UN, Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC),
Non-Aligned Movement (NAM), Group of 77 (G-77), and D-8 Organization for Economic
Cooperation (D-8). With regard to BRICS, Malaysia expressed its aspiration to become
a member of BRICS and its hope to work closely with Egypt and other BRICS Member
States towards this aspiration, as well as to advance the Global South agenda.
17. President Abdel Fattah Al Sisi expressed his profound gratitude to His Majesty
Sultan Ibrahim, the Government, and the people of Malaysia for the warm welcome and
gracious hospitality extended throughout the historic visit.
18. His Excellency Abdel Fattah Al Sisi, President of the Arab Republic of Egypt,
extended an invitation to His Majesty Sultan Ibrahim, King of Malaysia to visit Egypt.
