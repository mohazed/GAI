DRC, Israel Sign Cooperation Deals to Support Closer Economic Ties
Tuesday, 28 July 2026 09:05 Last modified on: 28 July 2026 09:06
email
facebook
linkedin
twitter
Whatsapp
The DRC and Israel signed two agreements to strengthen diplomatic cooperation.
The accords establish regular political consultations and cooperation between diplomatic academies.
The broader partnership could pave the way for closer economic, security and technology ties.
The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Israel have signed two memorandums of understanding aimed at strengthening diplomatic cooperation, marking another step in efforts by both countries to deepen bilateral relations.
The agreements were signed on July 27 in Jerusalem during a meeting between Congolese Foreign Minister Thérèse Kayikwamba Wagner and Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar. Wagner is on an official visit to Israel from July 26 to 29.
According to the DRC's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the agreements establish a framework for regular political and diplomatic consultations and cooperation between the diplomatic academies of both countries. The two ministers also agreed to increase coordination in multilateral forums on issues of common interest.
Their discussions focused on the security crises in eastern DRC and the Middle East. Wagner called for continued support for peace efforts in eastern Congo while emphasizing respect for the country's sovereignty and territorial integrity. Sa'ar welcomed the growing relationship between the two countries and highlighted the DRC's role as a member of the United Nations Security Council. He also pointed to Israel's support through its international development agency MASHAV and a three-ton medical aid shipment to help fight Ebola.
The Israeli foreign minister said the talks also covered the peace agreement between the DRC and Rwanda and stressed the importance of its full implementation to support regional peace and stability. Beyond diplomacy, the new agreements could lay the groundwork for broader cooperation in economic development, security and technology. For Kinshasa, the closer relationship is expected to strengthen its diplomatic capacity and help attract more investment, while Israel is seeking to expand its presence in Africa.
The agreements build on closer ties established over the past several years. Following Israeli President Isaac Herzog's visit to Kinshasa in November 2025, the two countries have expanded cooperation, including Israeli support for the Congolese armed forces through training and equipment. Bilateral trade, however, remains modest, totaling $6.7 million in 2025, according to the International Trade Centre.
