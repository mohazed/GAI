NATIONAL STATEMENT
BY THE MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS OF MALAYSIA
AT THE
GENERAL DEBATE OF THE 81ST SESSION OF THE UNITED NATIONS
GENERAL ASSEMBLY, NEW YORK, 26 SEPTEMBER 2026
Theme; “Restoring Trust, Managing Transformation: A United Nations
that Delivers for All”

PRESS COPY
Mr. President,
Excellencies,
Distinguished Delegates,
Ladies and Gentlemen,
1. I join others in congratulating my friend, His Excellency Dr.
Khalilur Rahman, on his election as President of the 81st Session
of the United Nations General Assembly.
2. You have Malaysia’s full support as you lead this Assembly
through a period of profound global uncertainty.
Mr. President,
3. Indeed, we are meeting at the most challenging time for the
UN in many years.
4. The world has become accustomed to crisis. Wars are
escalating. Major-power rivalry is sharpening. The international
arms-control architecture is under strain. New technologies are
transforming societies faster than our institutions can adapt.
5. But perhaps the greatest danger is not any one crisis. It is the
growing belief that the international system cannot solve them.
6. That is the trust deficit confronting the UN today.
7. And Malaysia believes we must confront it openly and honestly.
Trust can only be restored through concrete action.
8. When international law means what it says. And when the

PRESS COPY
people of the world can look to this Hall, and believe that their
voices still matter.
Mr. President,
9. We must remember that the UN is not merely here for the
aftermath of war, but to prevent war from taking place.
10. Yet too often, we respond only after disaster has arrived.
11. We count the dead. We deliver supplies. We rebuild what has
been destroyed. And then we wait for the next crisis.
12. This cannot be the future of multilateralism. We must move
from reaction to prevention.
13. And nowhere is that task more urgent than in Gaza.
Mr. President,
14. Malaysia will not stand here and recite statistics that everyone
in this Hall already knows. We all know the scale of death in Gaza.
15. We know how many children, families, medics, aid workers,
and journalists have been slaughtered.
16. We also know that Gaza represents the highest number of
children made orphans, in a single conflict.
17. Malaysia, and many other Member States, have exhausted
every effort in the pursuit of justice for Gaza.

PRESS COPY
18. We have consistently called for immediate ceasefire and
unrestricted supply of aid.
19. We have supported humanitarian works in Palestine.
20. We have pushed for punitive action towards Israel.
21. And we have advocated for full Palestinian membership in this
Hall.
22. And yet, again and again, the collective will of this Assembly
has encountered the silencing effect of the veto.
23. Malaysia will speak plainly:
24. We are sick of it.
Mr. President,
25. Nowhere is the need for reform more critical than in the
Security Council. Nowhere are the shortcomings of the UN more
glaring than in the Security Council.
26. The exercise of the veto must be questioned and strongly
challenged by every single member of this General Assembly.
Particularly in cases of crimes against humanity.
27. Resolution 76/262 has already created an accountability
channel between the Council and this Assembly.
28. We must make full use of this Resolution as a launchpad for

PRESS COPY
greater accountability regarding the use of the veto.
29. Expanding the Council's membership alone will not be enough
to resolve its failures. It is the veto that must be challenged, and it
is the veto that must be abolished. We must begin the work now.
30. There is still time to save the global order. The paralysis of the
Security Council will not be the paralysis of the UN.
Mr. President,
31.It is fair to say that global trust in the UN's ability to deliver is
at an all-time low.
32. This, in turn, demolishes trust in the very idea of the
multilateral order.
33. The citizens we serve and represent are afraid, Excellencies.
34. They are watching the world order fragment.
35. They are witnessing the re-emergence of an arms race.
36. They have heard overt threats to use nuclear weapons.
37. And they are seeing their governments choose not to regulate
A.I., and the many ways it is being abused, but instead give it
more power.
38. In such chaotic times, the only way to course-correct is to
rebuild trust in the power of pro-active governance and

PRESS COPY
multilateralism.
Mr. President,
39. Trust comes from the way in which we, as world leaders, can
manage transformative forces.
40. For centuries, global power was concentrated in states. But
today, a new source of power has emerged.
41. A handful of private actors now control how billions of people
communicate, work, transact, and consume information.
42. Artificial intelligence is changing how economies operate, how
societies function, and how governments make decisions.
43. The opportunities are enormous. But so are the risks.
44. We must therefore ask a simple question: Who governs the
transformation?
45. Technology must serve humanity. Never the reverse.
46. And the answer cannot be that a small number of companies
or countries decide the rules for everyone else.
47. The UN must continue to empower Global South countries,
who are likely to be more greatly affected by unequitable A.I.
development, to participate in global discussions on A.I.
48. The Global South cannot simply become a destination for data

PRESS COPY
centres, while decisions about A.I. are made elsewhere.
49. We must be partners in innovation. Not merely customers and
markets. And not merely destinations for investment.
50. Partners, involved in the decisions that will shape our future.
51. Equitable decision-making, and equitable development.
52. That means managing the enormous demands that tech
infrastructure places on water, energy, land, and other resources,
so that the benefits of A.I. can be shared more broadly, across
borders.
Mr. President,
53. In our hyper-connected world today, indeed, very few things
stay where they begin. Technologies are rapidly shared, and they
can just as rapidly disrupt. Similarly, war can also travel beyond
borders.
54. Across West Asia, escalating conflict threatens some of the
world's most important routes for energy and trade, including the
Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandab.
55. The consequences of conflict do not stop at these shores. A
family in Southeast Asia has no part in these disputes. Yet that
family still pays the price, when food, fuel, and cost of living rise.
56. Malaysia therefore urges all parties to step back from
escalation.

PRESS COPY
57. Diplomacy has shown that dialogue is possible. That path
must not be abandoned.
58. Or we risk sending further shockwaves through global
markets, and needlessly bringing suffering to innocent families
around the world.
Mr. President,
59. Most nations in this Hall are not great powers. Yet decisions
taken by a few can shape the future of all.
60. We are the global majority. As I said earlier, we must not
simply live with the consequences of choices made elsewhere.
We must help shape those choices.
61. Middle powers, such as Malaysia, can help keep dialogue alive
when major powers cannot agree.
62. Regional organisations, such as ASEAN, can bring the
perspectives of their people into global debates. We must have
the confidence to speak for our regions, not be spoken for.
Mr. President,
63. I began by speaking about trust. Let me close where I began.
64. The question before us may appear simple, but in truth, it is
profound.

PRESS COPY
65. When people are suffering, and when the world looks
towards this Hall, will this organisation still answer the call for
which it was created?
66. Actions speak louder than words, my esteemed Colleagues.
67. We must restore trust. Manage transformation.
68. Reform institutions that no longer serve current realities.
69. And we must ensure that the rules of the global system apply
equally to all.
70. Malaysia will not turn away from the UN in its hour of
difficulty. We will not abandon multilateralism just because it is
imperfect.
71. We will do the work, to make it worthy of the trust placed in
it.
72. Because we believe in multilateralism, most of all, when the
alternative is a world ruled by power alone.
Excellencies,
73. Eighty years ago, the founders of this organisation resolved to
save succeeding generations from war.
74. We are those succeeding generations. And that promise now
rests in our hands.

PRESS COPY
75. The world needs a UN that delivers.
76. One that ensures that international law is truly international.
And guarantees that every region has a voice.
77. And above all, one that ordinary people can look to in their
darkest hour, and still believe that it will act. That the world has
not forgotten them.
Excellencies,
78. Before concluding, we would like to offer our most heartfelt
gratitude to Secretary General Antonio Guterres.
79. As this will be the final session of the General Assembly
during his tenure, we thank him for leading the UN with
unyielding dedication and patience through many unprecedented
global upheavals.
80. These are the qualities we will need to face our future as an
organisation.
81. And if there is anything that humanity has demonstrated
throughout our history, it is our capacity to adapt.
82. My esteemed Colleagues, let us adapt and prove ourselves
worthy of the promise entrusted to us.
Thank you.
