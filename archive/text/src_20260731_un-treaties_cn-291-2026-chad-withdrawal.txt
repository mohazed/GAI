ROME STATUTE OF THE INTERNATIONAL CRIMINAL COURT
ROME, 17 JULY 1998
CHAD: WITHDRAWAL1
The Secretary-General of the United Nations, acting in his capacity as depositary,
communicates the following:
The above action was effected on 27 July 2026, with:
(Translation) (Original: French)
… This decision was made after careful consideration of the experience obtained through the
objective review of the International Criminal Court’s operations since the Rome Statute entered into
force on 1 July 2002.
Indeed, in light of the hopes and ideals that guided the creation of this institution, the
Government of the Republic of Chad notes with regret that its actual operations have gradually strayed
from the original objectives, given its modest track record and inconsistent effectiveness. Furthermore,
other factors resulting from the breakdown of the world order have compounded these inherent flaws,
thereby dealing a fatal blow to the credibility of the International Criminal Court. The statistics
published by the Court itself are a telling illustration of this. In fact, according to the official data
published by the Court and updated as at 11 May 2026, the Rome Statute has 125 States Parties,
including 33 African States. Since it began its work, the Court has opened 13 investigations, the first of
which successively concerned Uganda, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Darfur (Sudan), the
Central African Republic, Kenya, Libya, Côte d’Ivoire, Mali and Burundi, before gradually opening
cases in other regions of the world. As of that same date, nine of the situations under investigation have
therefore involved African countries, compared with four situations opened in other regions, although
no significant concrete progress has been made.
The Office of the Prosecutor is now conducting only one preliminary examination, concerning
Nigeria, an African country. As of the same date, the Court had only seven detainees, six of whom are
being prosecuted in cases involving situations in Africa and only one in a case outside the African
continent. Taken as a whole, these objective factors explain the perception – widely shared within many
States and their people – that the Court’s judicial activity is persistently concentrated on the African
continent, giving the plausible impression that the Court is being used for political purposes.
At the same time, the Republic of Chad notes that national judicial systems and African
judicial mechanisms have reached a level of maturity that enables them to effectively prosecute
international crimes while respecting African sovereignty and national sovereignty. The experience of
the Extraordinary African Chambers, which tried former President Hissène Habré; the proceedings

brought before Chadian courts against several of his former associates; and the reparations measures
implemented for the benefit of the victims all serve as concrete examples of this development.
The Government of the Republic of Chad therefore believes that the principle of
complementarity enshrined in the Rome Statute now calls for greater recognition of the capabilities of
African national and regional courts. Our future generation deserves to inherit an international justice
system that is fair, balanced and free from subjective influences.
The withdrawal of the Republic of Chad from the Rome Statute in no way constitutes its
disengagement from the imperatives of justice and the fight against impunity, in accordance with the
other international instruments to which it is a Party. It reflects the sovereign decision of Chad to
prioritize a model based more on strengthening national and African capacities, drawing lessons from
the limitations and subjectivity revealed by the implementation of the Rome Statute…
***
The action shall take effect for Chad on 27 July 2027 in accordance with its article 127 (1)
which reads as follows:
“A State Party may, by written notification addressed to the Secretary-General of the United
Nations, withdraw from this Statute. The withdrawal shall take effect one year after the date of receipt
of the notification, unless the notification specifies a later date.”
