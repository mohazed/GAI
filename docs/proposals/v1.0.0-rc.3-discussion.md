**Title:** Proposed: methodology v1.0.0-rc.3, comment until 2026-12-12

The methodology of the Gaza Accountability Index is versioned, and every change is proposed in
public before it is merged (methodology page, Versioning and changelog). This discussion opens
the 14-day comment window for version 1.0.0-rc.3. It closes on **12 December 2026**. The proposal
is merged only after that date, and comments received here are answered in this thread.

Pull request: {PR} (branch `methodology/v1.0.0-rc.3`). It is built on version 1.0.0-rc.2 (pull
request #15, whose own discussion is open at the same time), so it contains rc.2's changes until
rc.2 is merged.

**What it is.** Version 1.0.0 is still a release candidate: the scores are computed and not yet
displayed on the site. Two questions found while the data was completed are answered here by a
rule, the same for every country. Each was written up as a proposal with its options and their
measured effect (`docs/proposals/b1-article-19.md` and `docs/proposals/a4-data-years.md` in the
repository); this version takes the recommended option of each.

**The two rule changes**

1. **UN General Assembly votes (B1): an absence of a state that had no vote under Article 19 of
   the Charter scores 0.** Under Article 19, a member whose arrears equal or exceed its
   contributions for the two preceding full years has no vote in the General Assembly unless the
   Assembly permits it to vote. Until now every absence scored −2, including those of states that
   had no vote. Each of these absences is now tied to the Secretary-General's letter under
   Article 19 in force on the date of the vote, archived with the index's other sources: 30
   absences by six states (Venezuela 12, Afghanistan 10, Bolivia 3, Sao Tome and Principe 3,
   Ecuador 1, Somalia 1). They score 0, and the country page keeps each one with its reason and
   the letter. The other 188 absences, by states that were entitled to vote, keep −2.
2. **Arms ordered from Israel (A4): each order year from 2024 is scored once, in the first SIPRI
   release the index imports that reports it.** The index began importing SIPRI data with the
   release of 9 March 2026. The orders of 2024 belonged to the release of March 2025, which was
   never imported, so they were not scored at all. They are now scored with those of 2025 in the
   release of 9 March 2026, one value per country, each year at its own tier. From the next
   release on, each release scores its own year, as before.

The version also states two points the build already applies: an UNRWA funding event (D3) of
2025 ends the passivity penalty until 31 December 2026 where it is a state's only qualifying
event; and scores are displayed only when none of the 193 scored entities has an unchecked
indicator, which the build now enforces. The changelog entry gives each change with its rationale
and its measured effect.

**Effect.** On 28 November 2026, over the 193 scored entities, compared with 1.0.0-rc.2:

- With the events published so far (the generated ones), 10 display scores move by 1 point or
  more; the file `diff.json` of the version lists each with the cause. Venezuela −22 → −12,
  Afghanistan −25 → −15, Bolivia −21 → −16 and Sao Tome and Principe −25 → −22 (votes); Slovakia
  −30 → −40, Austria −8 → −18, Azerbaijan +3 → −7, Kenya 0 → −5, Liberia −13 → −15 and the
  Philippines −2 → −4 (orders).
- With the hand-researched events awaiting review counted as if published, the numbers are
  provisional: the same 10 display scores move and 5 bands change. The pull request has the table
  of each country under rc.1, rc.2 and rc.3.

`diff.json` is computed again on the published data before the scores are displayed.

**How to comment.** Reply in this thread, or open an issue in the repository with the label
`methodology`. Please name the rule, and, where you can, a document. Two points are open in
particular: whether an absence under Article 19 should score 0 or keep −2 with its reason shown
(the fallback the proposal names), and whether the orders of 2024 should be scored at all, since
their own release was never imported.

**What happens next.** After 12 December 2026 the author decides, with the comments, whether to
merge the proposal as it stands, change it, or withdraw part of it; any change to the proposal
reopens the window for that part. The decision and its reasons are recorded in the changelog.
